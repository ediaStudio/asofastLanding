How I got app downloads without paying for ads

Published on 2026-09-09

I launched my first app on a Tuesday and checked the downloads page every hour for a week. By the end of the first month, the counter showed 11 installs. Two of them were me on my own devices, three were friends being polite, and the rest never opened the app twice. I had spent eight months building it, and I expected launch day to be the finish line. It turned out to be the start of the hardest part.

My first reaction was the same reaction every desperate developer has. I opened the Meta ads manager. The logic felt airtight: downloads are a numbers game, so buy some numbers. I set a ten dollar a day budget, then twenty when nothing happened. I tried TikTok ads because someone on Reddit said short videos were cheap. In six weeks I burned around four hundred dollars. The campaigns delivered installs, technically. People downloaded the app, opened it once, and uninstalled it. A few installs looked fake, the kind that traffic farms sell to people like me. The users who stayed cost more per install than the app made per user, and the moment I paused the budget, the downloads stopped. I was renting my audience by the day.

The worst part was that I could not stop. Every day without downloads felt like proof that the app was dead. I raised the bid, changed the creative, switched platforms, and convinced myself the next tweak would fix everything. It never did. What I refused to see was that the ads were pushing traffic at a listing that could not convert anyone, and that nobody could find that listing in the first place.

When someone searched the words my app should have ranked for, the store showed my competitors. Not me. My title was clever instead of descriptive. My description was a wall of text that buried the few keywords it contained. My screenshots looked nice and said nothing about what the app did. And the whole listing existed in English only, in stores where most searches happen in other languages. I was paying to push traffic toward a page that was invisible to anyone who typed the words that mattered.

So I stopped the ads and spent my evenings differently. I rebuilt the title around the two or three search terms people actually type. I rewrote the description so keywords appeared naturally and the first two lines did the selling. I made screenshots that showed the real screens with real text explaining the value. That work cost nothing except time, and it changed things in a way ads never had. Downloads started arriving with no budget behind them. Slow at first, then steady. Organic users came from search, they stayed, some of them left reviews, and the reviews pushed the listing higher, which brought more search traffic. That cycle compounds. Paid installs never compounded for me, they just stopped when the money did.

Later I learned how much the single language was costing me. Some languages bring volume, others bring money. When I translated the listing into a handful of markets, downloads appeared from countries I had never targeted, and one market converted to paying users at a rate that made the English traffic look sleepy. I saw the same lesson in a friend's football app: an English-only football app is invisible in Brazil and Turkey, exactly the places where the users live. If your app serves a niche, that niche has a language and a country, and your listing has to live there too.

The honest question is why I did not do this before launch. Because the tooling scared me off. The serious ASO platforms cost real money, from ASODesk at 29.99 dollars a month to AppTweak at 79, and Sensor Tower starts around 500 a month on quote. I had already paid Apple 99 dollars for the developer account. Paying a second rent to find out why nobody was downloading my app felt like paying to be insulted, so I skipped it and launched blind.

That is exactly the gap AsoFast fills. It generates the title, subtitle, description and keywords for your App Store and Google Play listings with AI, translates the listing into more than 80 languages, and provides screenshot templates so the visual layer says the right thing. When you are ready, it publishes directly to App Store Connect and Google Play from one place. Everything runs locally on your machine, so your credentials never leave your computer. It is open source under the MIT license and free, which matters when the alternative is another subscription you are not sure will pay for itself.

If you are in the spot I was in, sitting in the ads manager and watching money leave, close the tab. Look at your listing first. The traffic is already in the stores, typed into search bars every day. Your job is to be the answer that shows up when people type. AsoFast does the heavy lifting for free, and unlike an ad budget, the work keeps paying after you stop.
