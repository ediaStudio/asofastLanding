What is ASO and how to start

Published on 2026-08-14

App Store Optimization, or ASO, is the practice of improving your app listing so more people find it, understand it, and install it. It sits at the intersection of copywriting, design, keyword research, and analytics. If you have ever rewritten an app title, reordered screenshots, or tested a new description, you have already done ASO.

Many developers treat ASO as a mystical dark art. It is not. The core inputs are visible to anyone willing to look. On the App Store, those inputs include the app name, subtitle, keyword field, description, screenshots, preview video, ratings, and reviews. On Google Play, the title, short description, full description, screenshots, feature graphic, video, ratings, and reviews all matter. Each element gives the store signals about what your app is and who it is for.

The first thing to fix is clarity. A user should understand your app within three seconds of landing on the listing. That means the title and subtitle must communicate the category and the main benefit. The first screenshot should show the app in action, not a splash screen. The short description should explain the outcome in one sentence. If a visitor has to read the full description to understand what the app does, you have already lost most of them.

Keywords come next. Think about what a person would type into the store search bar when they need your app. Make a list of those words and phrases. Check what autocomplete suggests. Look at which apps currently rank for those terms. Read their metadata. Then place your strongest keywords in the title and subtitle, and use the remaining space for variations. On Google Play, weave keywords naturally through the description, because Google indexes that text.

Conversion is the other half of ASO. Getting impressions is useless if people do not install. Your screenshots are the biggest conversion lever. Each one should tell a single story. The first screenshot should answer "What does this app do?" The next screenshots should show how it works and why it is better than alternatives. Add short captions, keep the interface legible, and avoid clutter.

Ratings and reviews are harder to control, but they have a large impact. Encourage happy users to rate the app at the right moment, such as after they complete a key action. Respond to negative reviews with specific help. A steady stream of recent positive reviews signals quality to both the store algorithms and potential users.

Analytics complete the loop. App Store Connect and Google Play Console show impressions, page views, conversion rates, and download sources. Use that data to decide what to test next. If impressions are low, focus on keywords. If page views are high but conversion is low, focus on screenshots and description. If a specific country underperforms, consider localization.

Seasonal updates are an underrated part of ASO. Stores pay attention to freshness. A title or screenshot refresh around a holiday, back-to-school period, or major feature launch can improve visibility. You do not need a full redesign. Sometimes a single updated screenshot caption or a refreshed first screenshot is enough to signal that the app is actively maintained.

AsoFast was built to make this workflow less scattered. It gives you one place to draft metadata, generate screenshots, manage translations, and track your submission checklist. Because it is open source and runs locally, you can iterate on your listing as often as you want without racking up SaaS fees.

The best way to start is to audit your current listing today. Write down your top five keywords. Compare your first screenshot to the top three apps in your category. Read your last twenty reviews. Pick one change to make this week. ASO is a series of small improvements, not a single heroic rewrite. The developers who rank well are the ones who keep shipping better listings.
